# Narrative editing mode

Narrative editing is driven by scene intention, performance, dramatic beats, information control, and spatial/audio continuity. Do not organize footage by musical intensity alone. Music is optional during assembly and should not conceal weak performance or scene construction.

Before cutting:

1. Break the screenplay or outline into scenes, beats, character objectives, reversals, reveals, and required information.
2. Map sources and takes to scene/beat, including establishing shots, masters, over-shoulders, singles, POVs, reactions, inserts, and wild lines or ambience.
3. Record eyelines, axis and screen direction, action phase, entrances/exits, props, wardrobe, lighting/time, and performance continuity.
4. Select performance for truthful emotion and story clarity before pursuing technically perfect continuity. Document material continuity compromises.
5. Build the dialogue and production-sound spine first when the scene is dialogue-led. Preserve useful reactions, pauses, breaths, and off-screen information.

At each cut decide what must be shown, why the cut belongs at that moment, and what the next image changes. Use masters for orientation and resets; tighten coverage as pressure or intimacy increases; use reactions and inserts for information or emotional consequence, not decoration. Cut on action and respect eyelines when they improve continuity, but never let lower-level geometry destroy the stronger emotional or story choice.

For suspense, control when information is revealed. For action, maintain readable geography and action phase before increasing density. For parallel action, establish each location before accelerating cross-cutting.

After assembly, screen without temp music when possible, repair scene intention and performance, then add sound design and score.
