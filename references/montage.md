# Music-driven montage mode

The user supplies pre-curated footage and owns the first-pass decision about unwanted errors and functional repetition. Flag later concerns, but do not silently override that curation.

Background music is required before timing design, paper editing, or rendering. Footage indexing and review may proceed while music is missing.

After deep review:

1. Map the actual track: sections, pulse, accents, transitions, density changes, rise, release, climax candidates, ending behavior, and usable duration.
2. Derive a project-specific visual progression from purpose, footage vocabulary, and music. Do not impose the arc of any earlier montage.
3. Resolve functionally similar shots deliberately. Prefer the strongest realization unless repetition has a clear structural meaning.
4. Write an EDL containing source in/out, visual role, music cue, transition intent, and selection reason.
5. Make synchronization perceptible through action phases, impacts, camera changes, reveals, motion direction, and visual density—not through indiscriminate cutting on every beat.
6. Preserve contrast: sustained peaks require quieter preparation, readable shot duration, and controlled release.

Packaging must clarify authorship, purpose, or structure. It must not cover weak shot selection. Design titles, graphics, grade, and sound treatment for the current project only.
